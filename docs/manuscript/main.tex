\begin{abstract}
Federated learning (FL) enables collaborative cyber-defense model training across unmanned aircraft without centralizing sensitive telemetry, but compromised edge nodes can poison global models through scaled gradients, Byzantine updates, backdoors, and label manipulation.
We present \emph{Adversarially-Conditioned Federated Learning} (ACFL), a server-side defense that combines counterfactual update validation, mission-context trust-weighted aggregation, and optional inference-time spoofing detection.
Evaluated on software-in-the-loop ArduPilot packet traces with a resource-budgeted TinyPacketCNN ($\sim$14k parameters) over ten FL seeds, ACFL is the only method that preserves mission classification F1 under Byzantine attack (0.699 vs.\ 0.018 for FedAvg), improves gradient-scaling robustness (+0.257 F1), and reduces backdoor trigger success by $\approx$85\% relative to FedAvg (and below Krum) while matching clean-task utility.
Layer ablations confirm that counterfactual probes are required for malicious-client attribution (trust AUC 0.5 without Layer~1) and that the full three-layer stack preserves both FL utility and inference-time spoofing detection under Byzantine poisoning (Layer~3 AUROC 0.94 vs.\ 0.54 for FedAvg).
At $n{=}5$ model replacement, ACFL with Layer~1 trust and attack-scoped hard rejection reaches 0.607 mean F1 versus 0.286 for Krum under a fair minimum-quorum-four comparison.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Collaborative intrusion detection on drone swarms increasingly relies on federated learning~\cite{mcmahan2017fedavg,lim2020federated} so that raw packet traces need not leave the airframe.
Yet the same decentralization exposes the global model to compromised participants who can submit scaled gradients, arbitrary Byzantine noise, backdoor triggers, corrupted labels, or full model-replacement updates.
Classical Byzantine-robust aggregators---coordinate median, trimmed mean, and Krum~\cite{blanchard2017krum,yin2018trimmed,chen2017median}---assume sufficiently large benign cohorts and often fail in bandwidth-limited, low-participation avionics settings where only two or three edge nodes may be online per round.
We propose ACFL, which conditions aggregation on adversarial \emph{scenarios} (counterfactual probes) and mission-context telemetry, not only on update geometry.

Our evaluation targets a realistic SITL packet-classification workload: a $\sim$14k-parameter TinyPacketCNN trained on MAVLink-derived features from ArduPilot missions with held-out eval missions per agent.
Across a five-defender $\times$ five-attack matrix (ten seeds on a three-client swarm, plus five-client scaling at 20\% malicious), ACFL is the only method that preserves high F1 under Byzantine attack while remaining competitive on gradient scaling, slightly leading Krum on label flip at $n{=}5$, and leading high-utility defenders on backdoor trigger suppression.
Krum matches ACFL on backdoor clean F1 and remains competitive on label flip, but is seed-volatile on Byzantine and gradient scaling and collapses on several model-replacement seeds when minimum quorum is four; FedAvg collapses on Byzantine poisoning and can poison the inference-time detector.
ACFL therefore combines backdoor trigger leadership with \emph{cross-attack} stability and interpretable trust attribution.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item A three-layer server-side defense (update validation, trust-weighted aggregation, inference detection) integrated with the drift-fairroad FL testbed.
  \item Layer~1/2 gating that separates trust telemetry from aggregation penalties, avoiding benign-client suppression under Byzantine attack.
  \item A reproducible drone benchmark matrix: five defenders $\times$ five learning-plane attacks $\times$ ten seeds on SITL packet data, plus five-client scaling at the same seed protocol.
  \item Empirical evidence that ACFL provides \emph{cross-attack} robustness where specialized baselines collapse or are seed-volatile (e.g., Krum on Byzantine/gradient scaling; FedAvg on Byzantine), with ablations clarifying Layer~1's role for attribution versus soft aggregation gating.
\end{itemize}

The remainder of this paper describes the ACFL architecture (Section~\ref{sec:method}), reports the benchmark and ablation results (Section~\ref{sec:experiments}), and discusses limitations and deployment implications (Sections~\ref{sec:related}--\ref{sec:conclusion}).

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Federated learning and edge deployment.}
FedAvg~\cite{mcmahan2017fedavg} established communication-efficient decentralized training; subsequent work addresses statistical heterogeneity~\cite{li2020fedprox,kairouz2021advances} and edge deployment constraints~\cite{lim2020federated,nguyen2021federated}.
Our SITL drone benchmark targets the low-participation, bandwidth-limited regime relevant to companion-computer FL on small UAS rather than datacenter-scale cross-device FL.

\paragraph{Byzantine-robust federated learning.}
Krum~\cite{blanchard2017krum} and Multi-Krum select geometrically central updates; coordinate-wise median and trimmed mean~\cite{yin2018trimmed,chen2017median} discard extreme dimensions or a fraction of outliers under analyses that assume a benign majority and roughly independent perturbations.
Recent analyses extend guarantees to non-convex settings~\cite{allenzhu2021byzantine}, but empirical poisoning studies show that geometry-only defenses remain vulnerable to model-replacement and backdoor strategies tailored to the aggregator~\cite{bhagoji2019analyzing,shejwalkar2022manipulating,fang2020local}.
Server-side trust bootstrapping~\cite{cao2021fltrust} and sybil-aware analyses~\cite{fung2020limitations} motivate validation beyond pairwise distances---ACFL's counterfactual probes and mission-context weighting follow this direction while retaining Krum-style shortlists as attack-scoped geometry gates.
In our three-client Byzantine results, classical aggregators collapse near FedAvg; at five clients Krum remains seed-volatile on Byzantine and gradient-scaling attacks while ACFL stays low-variance.

\paragraph{Backdoors, data poisoning, and model replacement.}
Backdoor attacks embed trigger-activated misbehavior while preserving clean accuracy~\cite{gu2017badnets,bagdasaryan2020backdoor,xie2019dba,sun2019can}; label-flip poisons local supervision; model replacement submits scaled anti-direction updates~\cite{baruch2019little,bhagoji2019analyzing,fang2020local}.
Defenses include robust aggregation, server-side validation, and post-training mitigation~\cite{cao2021fltrust,nguyen2022flame}.
ACFL's Layer~1 probe set plays a validation role with scenario-conditioned harm scores, then routes attack-specific gates: trigger veto and post-aggregation scrub for backdoors, shortlist-clean geometry for label flip, and hard norm rejection for model replacement.
Empirically, Krum remains a strong pure-geometry baseline on label flip, while ACFL leads on backdoor trigger $P(\text{attack})$ among high-utility defenders and on model-replacement F1 at $n{=}5$ under a fair minimum-quorum comparison.
Negative ablations (probe-aware soft cap; soft-only model-replacement gating; clean-blend shortlists) document when probe-derived signals help versus when hard exclusion or single-update selection is required.

\paragraph{UAS cyber defense and network telemetry.}
Unmanned aircraft cyber-defense research evaluates GNSS spoofing, ADS-B vulnerabilities, autopilot protocol weaknesses, and network-attack injection on SITL/HIL corpora~\cite{humphreys2008spoofing,tippenhauer2011gps,strohmeier2011adsb,yaacoub2020uav,ernst2021uavids,hoang2019mavlink,shakhatreh2019uav}.
Packet- and flow-level anomaly detection provides complementary baselines for mission telemetry~\cite{chandola2009anomaly}.
The drift-fairroad stack supplies agent--mission FL splits, packet-window classifiers, and network-attack injection that we extend with a reproducible ACFL server harness, TinyPacketCNN resource budgeting, Layer~3 spoofing detection, and seeded multi-defender benchmarks.
We report operational metrics (time-to-detect, false alarms per hour, CPU inference latency) alongside AUROC/F1 so that FL robustness claims remain grounded in mission continuity for aerospace deployments.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

ACFL shows that adversarially conditioned trust---counterfactual probes, mission-context weighting, and attack-scoped geometry---can preserve federated cyber-defense utility under learning-plane attacks that defeat both FedAvg and standard robust aggregators in small drone swarms.
On a three-client, 33\% malicious benchmark, ACFL is the only defender that maintains mission F1 under Byzantine attack (0.699 vs.\ 0.018 FedAvg) while improving gradient-scaling robustness (+0.257 F1) and cutting backdoor trigger $P(\text{attack})$ from 0.339 to 0.052 ($\approx$85\% relative to FedAvg; below Krum's 0.187) without sacrificing clean-task F1.
At five clients and 20\% malicious, ACFL remains low-variance on Byzantine and gradient scaling, matches Krum on backdoor clean F1 while lowering trigger $P$ to 0.059 vs.\ 0.177, slightly leads Krum on label flip (0.751 vs.\ 0.746) under shortlist-clean geometry, and leads model replacement (0.607 vs.\ 0.286) when minimum quorum four enables attack-scoped hard rejection.
Standalone Layer~3 spoofing detection on held-out SITL missions reaches AUROC 0.968 and F1 0.916 with sub-millisecond CPU inference; the full stack's inference benefit is clearest when FL poisoning breaks the global model (Byzantine), not when FedAvg retains a usable checkpoint.
Limitations include gray-box evaluation only, thin label-flip margins versus Krum, and soft blending under sparse late rounds.
Releasing harness scripts and seeded benchmarks supports reproducibility; future work includes white-box adaptation to probes and gates, broader seed sweeps, and hardware-in-the-loop validation.
